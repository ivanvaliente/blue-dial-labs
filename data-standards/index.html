Canonical identity

The reference is the organizing anchor.

Listings and source identifiers change. A stable canonical identity allows observations from different sources and times to remain comparable without assuming every listing represents the same object.

Integration vocabulary

Seller is not channel is not source.

These roles can coincide, but they answer different questions. Preserving the distinction supports attribution, licensing, deduplication, seller evaluation, and meaningful comparison.

Who offers it?

Seller

The party that owns or offers the inventory: a dealer, retailer, brand, auction house, or private seller.

Where is it offered?

Sales channel

The venue where the collector can transact: a dealer website, marketplace, auction platform, showroom, or private market.

How did we learn?

Source

The route through which Blue Dial Labs learned about the offer: an API, affiliate feed, direct partner feed, licensed provider, or other permitted input.

Example

A dealer may be the seller, a marketplace the sales channel, and the marketplace's approved API the source.

Useful integration fields

Context makes offers comparable.

No single source must provide every field. Reliable, attributable signals are most useful when they improve identity, availability, economics, and the collector's understanding of transaction terms.

Evidence controls

Useful data still needs boundaries.

01

Preserve provenance

Source, source identifiers, timestamps, and relevant usage terms should remain traceable through normalization.

02

Retain history where permitted

Price and availability changes should not blindly erase earlier observations when applicable rights allow historical retention.

03

Gate low-confidence matches

Incomplete or fuzzy identity evidence can support investigation, but it must not silently drive high-confidence alerts, assignments, or recommendations.
