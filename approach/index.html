Our approach

Good decisions depend on disciplined evidence.

Standard Time Labs separates product identity, source observations, normalized economics, confidence, and collector context so weak evidence does not turn into false certainty.

Why the architecture matters

Technical discipline should produce visible decision quality.

The system is not rigorous for its own sake. Each structural choice exists to prevent a specific class of bad collector decision.

Correct identity

So a collector does not compare the wrong reference or variant as if it were equivalent.

Comparable economics

So a lower headline price does not masquerade as a better deal when shipping, geography, fees, warranty, or other supported terms differ.

Confidence-aware matching

So incomplete or fuzzy evidence cannot silently produce high-confidence alerts, valuations, assignments, or recommendations.

Historical evidence

So today's price or availability does not erase yesterday's market context when retention rights permit history to be preserved.

Technical teams can review the canonical identity hierarchy, seller/channel/source distinctions, and integration fields separately.

Evidence architecture

Keep observation, interpretation, and decision distinct.

A source observation is not a normalized offer. A normalized offer is not automatically relevant to a particular collector. Keeping those layers separate makes corrections possible and prevents weak upstream evidence from becoming false downstream confidence.

Observed

Source evidence

What a source reported at a particular time, with source and timestamp retained where rights allow.

  • Original identifiers
  • Price and availability
  • Source and timestamp

Interpreted

Normalized offer

A comparable representation with canonical identity, economics, seller, channel, terms, and confidence.

  • Reference match
  • Condition and terms
  • Comparable components

Contextual

Collector decision

An evaluation against one collector's intent, constraints, supported economics, and confidence requirements.

  • Fit and thresholds
  • Estimated delivered cost
  • Action and confidence

Operating rules

What the system refuses to blur.

01

Retain history where permitted

Later changes should not blindly erase earlier market evidence when source rights and commercial terms permit historical retention.

02

Gate low-confidence matches

Low-confidence identity evidence may support investigation, but it must not silently drive high-confidence collector actions.

03

Protect collector context

Buying thresholds, preferences, rejection behavior, and collection information are private by default and shared only through deliberate permission.

04

Separate revenue from advice

Commercial relationships can support operations. They cannot improve source inclusion, rank, recommendation, or stated confidence.

A deliberate boundary

Explainable enough to trust. Restrained enough to be honest.

Matching confidence and recommendation confidence answer different questions. Both remain bounded by the quality of the underlying evidence.
