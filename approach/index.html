Our approach

Structure before scale. Confidence before certainty.

Watch-market intelligence is only useful when the underlying identity, evidence, economics, and confidence can withstand scrutiny. We are designing the product around those constraints from the start.

Canonical identity

The reference is the organizing anchor.

Listings come and go; a watch's canonical product identity is more durable. Organizing evidence around the correct reference and meaningful variant allows observations from different sources and moments to become comparable without pretending every listing is the same object.

  1. 01
    Brand

    The manufacturer or recognized watch brand.

  2. 02
    Collection / Family

    The named product family or collection.

  3. 03
    Model

    The conceptual model within that collection.

  4. 04
    Reference

    The core canonical commercial product identity.

  5. 05
    Variant

    A stable distinction beneath a reference where the difference matters.

Evidence architecture

Keep observation, interpretation, and decision distinct.

A source observation is not a normalized offer. A normalized offer is not automatically relevant to a particular collector. Keeping those layers separate makes corrections possible and prevents weak upstream evidence from becoming false downstream confidence.

Observed

Source evidence

What a source reported at a particular time, retained when rights and terms permit.

  • Original identifiers
  • Price and availability
  • Source and timestamp

Interpreted

Normalized offer

A comparable representation with canonical identity, economics, seller, channel, and confidence.

  • Reference match
  • Condition and terms
  • Comparable components

Contextual

Collector decision

An evaluation in the context of one collector's intent, constraints, and supported economics.

  • Fit and thresholds
  • Estimated Delivered Cost
  • Action and confidence

Six working principles

Built for decision quality.

01

Retain history where permitted

Later price or availability changes should not blindly erase earlier market evidence. Retention always remains subject to source rights and commercial terms.

02

Gate low-confidence matches

Incomplete or fuzzy identity evidence may support investigation, but it must not silently drive high-confidence valuations, alerts, assignments, or recommendations.

03

Normalize the economics

Advertised price, shipping, currency, fees, geography, and supported tax or duty context are different inputs. Estimated Delivered Cost is context-specific—not universal market truth.

04

Protect collector context

Buying thresholds, preferences, rejection behavior, and collection information are private by default and should be disclosed only through deliberate, permissioned choices.

05

Prefer quality over count

A smaller set of reliable, complementary sources can provide better decision evidence than a larger stream of poorly identified or economically incomparable listings.

06

Separate revenue from advice

Commercial relationships can support operations. They cannot improve a source's inclusion, rank, recommendation, or stated confidence.

A deliberate boundary

Explainable enough to trust. Restrained enough to be honest.

We do not intend to claim certainty that the evidence cannot support. Matching confidence and recommendation confidence answer different questions, and both should remain bounded by the quality of their inputs.
