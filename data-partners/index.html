Data & Partners

Responsible access. Useful evidence.

Standard Time Labs is evaluating structured data relationships that can support trustworthy watch intelligence. We prefer authorized, durable paths over brittle or unclear access.

Preferred acquisition paths

Sanctioned data first.

We do not intend to build the core product around indiscriminate or unauthorized scraping. Every production source should be evaluated for access rights, retention and redistribution terms, data quality, coverage, reliability, rate limits, and operational risk.

  1. 01

    Official APIs

    Documented marketplace, retailer, manufacturer, or partner interfaces with clear usage terms.

    Preferred

  2. 02

    Affiliate or product feeds

    Structured catalog and offer data made available through approved publisher or commerce relationships.

  3. 03

    Direct retailer or dealer feeds

    Partner-authorized data supplied through an agreed API, scheduled feed, or other structured exchange.

  4. 04

    Licensed commercial data

    Appropriately licensed datasets with rights suitable for the intended analysis, retention, and display.

  5. 05

    Compliant site monitoring

    Narrow monitoring only where legally, contractually, technically, and commercially appropriate.

Integration vocabulary

Seller is not channel is not source.

These roles may coincide, but they answer different questions. Preserving the distinction supports licensing, attribution, deduplication, seller evaluation, and meaningful comparison.

Who offers it?

Seller

The party that owns or offers the inventory: a dealer, retailer, brand, auction house, or private seller.

Where is it offered?

Sales channel

The venue where the collector can transact: a dealer website, marketplace, auction platform, showroom, or private market.

How did we learn?

Source

The route through which Standard Time Labs learned about the offer: an API, affiliate feed, direct partner feed, licensed provider, or other permitted input.

Example

A dealer may be the seller, a marketplace the sales channel, and the marketplace's approved API the source.

Useful integration fields

Context makes offers comparable.

No single source must provide every field. We are interested in reliable, attributable signals that improve identity, availability, economics, and the collector's understanding of transaction terms.

Identity

  • Manufacturer reference
  • Model and variant
  • Structured attributes
  • Source identifiers

Offer

  • Seller
  • Sales channel
  • Condition
  • Availability

Economics

  • Price and currency
  • Shipping
  • Geography
  • Known fees

Terms

  • Warranty
  • Returns
  • Fulfillment
  • Membership requirements

Evidence

  • Observation timestamp
  • Update timestamp
  • Provenance
  • Usage and retention rights

Partnership principles

Clear terms. Controlled use. Neutral outcomes.

01

Rights-aware by design

Access, retention, transformation, attribution, and display should reflect the applicable agreement and source policy.

02

Minimum necessary data

We prefer focused integrations that capture the fields needed for useful collector decisions without unnecessary data collection.

03

No purchased preference

A direct relationship or commercial payment can make data available; it cannot make an offer rank better or become the recommended action.
